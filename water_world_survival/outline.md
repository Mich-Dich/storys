### Days 1–2: Awakening and First Escape

- **Day 1, 06:23 — Awakening**  
  John wakes in the bow cabin of the *Daydream*. White walls, blue accent lighting, gentle rocking. The mechanical voice announces the Ocean Game. Through the porthole, a black fog bank on the horizon. He rises, steps up the 0.6 m rise to the command station, sees his laptop, the familiar nav screens. Presses start. The 10 kW motor hums at 3 kW draw, solar pushing 3.8 kW back. Chart plotter shows 48 hours of open water. He sets a course away from the fog.

- **Day 1, 06:45 — Chat Chaos**  
  The interface chat scrolls with panic: people on battleships they can't start, a submarine with a stuck hatch, a girl on an alien ship, a man on a plastic island floaty. John reads silently. Some post images. He notices early mentions: a fishing trawler reports they found a floating barrel of diesel, *“At least we can drift with engines on standby.”* John files that away—fuel exists, but it’s a scavenger’s game. His own boat needs none.

- **Day 1, Full Tour**  
  He explores: the master cabin, the step-up to the command station with desk and sofa, then down to the galley, bathroom, workbench with tools, aft porch, swim platform. Everything matches his plans, except a biometric safe under the sofa. He sets his fingerprint.

- **Day 1, Evening**  
  Pasta on the aft porch. Chat reports first death. John switches to night mode (1 kW cruise) and sleeps.

- **Day 2 — Systems Check and Driftwood**  
  John spends the day calibrating instruments, confirming watermaker output (30 L/h). He hauls in a drifting wooden pallet—useful for future projects. The chat reveals more fuel finds: someone on an island found a cache of fuel barrels half-buried in sand. *“It’s like the world wants us to move, but barely,”* they comment. John agrees.

---

### Days 3–5: First Salvage, First Upgrade, Monster Reveal

- **Day 3 — Shallow Seamount and First Dive**  
  Sonar picks up a rise to 8 m. John anchors briefly, free-dives, recovers aluminium cleats, stainless rail, a waterlogged GPS from a sunken runabout. Interface logs resources. He raises anchor and moves 5 miles before night.

- **Day 3, Evening — First Upgrade: Wind Generator**  
  He experiments with the Upgrade tab. Vague “wind generator” costs too much; a precisely designed 400W turbine using his exact salvage costs 0.8 kg copper, 2 kg aluminium, electronic scrap. He has enough. The metals dissolve into a shimmering cloud and reform as a sleek turbine in under a minute. He mounts it; by morning, it’s trickling 0.5 kW.

- **Day 4 — Monster Horror Unfolds**  
  Chat erupts: an anchored cruiser, stationary for days, attacked by a massive tentacled thing. Photo shows a pale arm lined with hooks. The player greys out. More attacks on stationary vessels. John analyses timestamps—all 4+ days motionless. He resolves to never anchor overnight without moving 10 miles. The fuel find chatter intensifies: desperate players are now diving near islands for barrel caches to stay mobile.

- **Day 5 — Refining the Routine**  
  John watches sunrise, coffee in hand. He starts a detailed logbook: daily mileage, power balance, fog sightings. His solar-powered independence is already a quiet fortress.

---

### Days 6–10: System Mastery, First Trade, Hull Growth

- **Day 6 — The Detail Principle**  
  Experiment: a vaguely defined rod holder costs 2 kg aluminium; a fully specified design costs 0.3 kg. John now meticulously blueprints every idea before crafting, saving mental templates.

- **Day 7 — First Trade**  
  A sailor offers a 200W solar panel for water. John trades 20 L of water (surplus) for the panel and charge controller. Instant materialisation. He mounts the panel, boosting solar to 5.2 kW peak. He remains silent but logs the deal.

- **Day 9–10 — First Major Modification: Hull Extension**  
  With salvaged fibreglass and wood, John designs a 2-metre stern lengthening. He activates fabrication: the boat shimmers and elongates to 16 m over 30 seconds, porch shifting aft, new bulkheads forming. *Daydream* is now roomier, more efficient.

---

### Days 11–15: Mapping Fog, Cryptic Encounter, Sleeper Speedboat

- **Day 11–12 — Fog Mapping**  
  John compiles a fog movement log, noticing patterns linked to currents. He trades a simplified map for rare earth magnets, eyeing a larger turbine.

- **Day 13 — The Old-Timer’s Trade (Revised)**  
  John receives a private message from a user named *Anne*. No greeting, just: *“I have coordinates for a shallow seamount with high-grade salvage: rare earths, broken 50 kW electric motor, lithium cells. I need a specific micro-inverter, model XYZ, to repair my own system. You had one on offer yesterday. Trade?”*  
  John checks the coordinates—distant but reachable, and suspiciously perfect for his needs. The micro-inverter she wants is rare, pulled from a wreck. He replies: *“Agreed, but how do you know that site? Those parts are beyond standard salvage.”*  
  Anne: *“I’ve been here long enough to map more than fog.”* A pause, then: *“413 days, to be exact. You’re new. Your trades are too fresh. But you’re not stupid. The cycle will bring more like you in 387 days.”*  
  John’s blood chills. 413 days. 400 days before his arrival. The 400-day cycle is real. He trades the inverter for the coordinates. Before closing the chat, Anne adds: *“Don’t broadcast this. The old ones like me don’t want crowds.”* John simply replies: *“Understood.”* He now knows the world is older than the panic suggests, and he has a treasure site to plunder.

- **Day 14 — Salvage at Anne’s Site**  
  He reaches the coordinates: a seamount at 12 m depth, scattered with ancient-looking wreckage—maybe a previous player’s ambitious electric catamaran. He recovers copper windings, neodymium magnets, a salvageable 50 kW motor core, and 40 high-capacity lithium cells. His resource stockpile jumps enormously.

- **Day 15 — Motor Upgrade**  
  Using the new magnets and copper, he designs a compact 20 kW motor, water-cooled, with dual-mode efficiency. The cloud rebuilds his drive unit. That afternoon, a test run: 5 knots at 2 kW, but a sudden 12-knot sprint at full 20 kW. The “sleeper speedboat” is taking shape.

---

### Days 16–20: Speed and the Second Wave

- **Day 16–18 — Battery Expansion and Hull Tuning**  
  The lithium cells from Anne’s site, plus trades, allow him to expand the bank to 40 kWh. He extends the boat to 18 m, integrating the larger motor smoothly. Top speed now 15 knots. He remains frugal: 4 knots cruising still uses barely 3 kW.

- **Day 19–20 — New Fog Surge**  
  A rapid fog bank traps a flotilla that stayed anchored too long near a fuel-rich island (they were collecting barrels). Many die. John marks the island’s coordinates with a warning in his personal chart, but doesn’t intervene. The fuel barrel mechanic proves both a lifeline and a trap—it encourages dangerous stationary gathering.

---

### Days 21–25: The Lonely Legend Grows

- **Day 21 — Alliance Refusal**  
  A fleet leader offers protection. John declines, but later trades a small solar generator to a struggling sailor in that fleet for more lithium cells. His neutrality remains.

- **Day 23 — Public Fog Map**  
  He releases a simplified fog chart for free, purely to stabilise the trade economy. Chat names him “The Electric Ghost.” He still never posts his boat.

- **Day 24–25 — Fine-Tuning**  
  John crafts an underwater foil to reduce drag, boosting range by 15%. The boat now cruises effortlessly at 5 knots on 2.5 kW. He’s practically unstoppable.

---

### Days 26–30: Vacation Amid Apocalypse

- **Day 27 — Fog Ambush**  
  A fog bank changes speed unexpectedly, trapping him in a shallow channel. He runs at 14 knots for 20 minutes, silent motor whining softly, escaping with metres to spare. He crafts a radar-reflective fog buoy for early warning.

- **Day 29 — Reflection**  
  On the aft porch, eating fresh fish, he journals: *“I’m not a survivor—I’m living my dream in a world that happens to be lethal. The fuel-seekers fight for barrels; I drink sun.”* He reads chat: a fishing vessel found a sunken tanker and is lifting barrels with a jury-rigged crane, ecstatic. The world provides, but it’s a brutal rhythm.

- **Day 30 — 720 Hours**  
  The system notes his starter provisions would have ended; he never noticed. He’s mapping an 8 kW solar awning and a silent underwater drive system. In 370 days, new players will arrive. By then, his boat will be a 20-metre phantom, and he’ll have a 10-metre trade boat ready for an alien ship.
